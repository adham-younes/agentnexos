# Agent systems built around your business

Agentnexos engineers enterprise agent systems. We connect organizational knowledge and business systems to tasks an agent can complete, from research and analysis to preparing outputs and performing authorized actions. Each system is defined around the working context, decision ownership, and the result your team needs.

## The knowledge a task needs, where the work happens

A policy lives in a document, an order status in a system, and the history of a relationship with another team. An employee needs to assemble that picture before acting. System design starts with approved sources, their business definitions and relationships, then makes relevant context available within the user's access rights.

Answers are grounded in retrieved material, with their sources visible. When sources conflict or essential information is missing, the system identifies the gap and returns it to the responsible person. [Enterprise knowledge](/en/solutions/knowledge)

## From a request to a result in the workflow

The agent interprets the request and identifies the knowledge and tools it needs. The workflow engine manages sequence and state, separating a draft from a sent message and a record read from a record update. Specialist roles can support tasks that need independent analysis and review; simple tasks follow a short path.

We define an observable end to the work: an answer with sources, a supplier comparison, a case file, or an action recorded in the connected system. The design covers missing inputs, failed connections, and stopping before a sensitive action. [System architecture](/en/platform)

## Control is part of execution design

The organization defines what the agent can read, prepare, and change. Approval is bound to the action and its inputs; changed inputs require a new assessment. The operational record is designed to connect the source, policy decision, tool result, and evidence of completion.

We test ambiguous requests, conflicting sources, unauthorized access attempts, and lost responses from external systems. The level of autonomy follows evaluation and review with the process owner. [Governance and security](/en/security)

## How the system reaches operation

We define the first process, its data sources, and acceptance criteria. A bounded pilot is compared with the current method, then integrations and permissions expand according to what testing demonstrates. Delivery includes workflow definitions, tests, operating guidance, and agreed responsibilities. [Delivery approach](/en/methodology)

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you work with our existing systems?
Integration discovery covers interfaces, versions, permissions, and data constraints. It establishes what can connect and what needs adjustment; an untested connection is never assumed.

### How do you choose models and architecture?
We evaluate the task in its required language and sources, then compare response time, cost, and deployment requirements. Retrieval, memory, knowledge graphs, and multiple agents are chosen according to the work.

### Does a person approve every step?
Review depends on the impact of the action and organizational policy. A system can gather information or prepare drafts without approving each step, while pausing before actions that require authorization.

### What can we try here?
The public process design assistant and local project brief. Enterprise integrations and execution tools require their own project, configuration, and tests.

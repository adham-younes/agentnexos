# Engineering services from business requirements to operation

Engagements can begin with one process, knowledge preparation, or a failing integration. Scope, deliverables, and acceptance tests are defined before implementation; the organization selects the appropriate level of access and execution.

## Process and architecture design

We review how work arrives, what completion requires, where steps stall, and who owns the decision. The model's role is separated from fixed rules, tool permissions, and escalation paths. The output is a workflow map and task contract connecting data and authority with the intended result.

## Enterprise knowledge preparation

We identify approved sources, versions, and access rights, and organize the terms, entities, and relationships the work needs. Scope includes retrieval design and evaluation, source conflicts, outdated content, and missing evidence. RAG or a deeper knowledge representation is selected according to the questions the system must answer.

## Agent and workflow development

We build the interface, role coordination, tool connections, and state management. Structured outputs, follow-up requests, changed inputs, and failure cases are tested. Persistence, resumption, and recovery follow the requirements of the process.

## System and tool integration

We assess APIs, identity, and usage limits, then build connections with defined actions. Each connection includes input validation, separate read and write permissions, timeouts, and result verification. Duplicate prevention is tested before consequential external actions run.

## Evaluation and governance

We prepare domain acceptance cases and cases for conflict, incomplete input, and unauthorized access. We review the evidence shown to users, autonomy boundaries, and the contribution of models and tools to the result. Findings become repeatable cases for each release.

## Delivery and operation

Deployment, support, updates, and retention responsibilities are agreed within the project. Delivery includes documentation, definitions, tests, and failure-handling guidance so the team can maintain the system and change its scope deliberately.

### What starts the scoping discussion

A recurring process, its owner, existing systems, and a fictional input/output example. Access, language, and deployment constraints can be shared without secrets or customer records in the first contact.

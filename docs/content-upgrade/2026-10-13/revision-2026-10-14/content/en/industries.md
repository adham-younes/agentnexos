# Solutions shaped by the sector's working context

Agent value depends on the data, constraints, and responsibilities of the sector. We define the process first, then choose its knowledge, tools, and review points. These examples describe possible development scopes; they are not customer case studies or ready-made sector products.

## Commerce and distribution

Sales and operations need catalog, pricing, inventory, customer terms, and order status together. An assistant can prepare an offer or explain supply status from connected sources, showing what needs approval before a commercial commitment.

The project defines handling of old prices, changed stock, and customer-specific terms. Pricing and credit exceptions stay with their authorized owners. Testing covers offer completeness, price-source accuracy, and inventory freshness.

## Energy and industrial services

Office and operational work depends on reports, contracts, work orders, and versioned technical guidance. Specialist knowledge can help maintenance and procurement teams assemble context and prepare review files or operational briefings.

Office decision support is distinguished from physical asset control. Devices and control systems are not an automatic integration scope and require separate definition and assessment. Technical references need a current version and an approval owner.

## Construction and project management

Project information spans contracts, drawings, correspondence, site reports, and requests. A system can connect a question to its clauses and references, prepare a gap log, compare versions, or assemble a change file.

Outputs distinguish document facts from system proposals. Contractual and financial approvals remain with authorized people. A useful initial scope produces a file that a specialist can compare with its originals.

## Professional services

Consulting and service teams spend time gathering knowledge, coordinating delivery, and following up on client requirements. Knowledge can be organized to prepare a briefing or delivery draft with sources and unresolved details.

Access follows project and team boundaries; one client's content must not appear in another client's task. Acceptance covers attribution, requirements completeness, and correct data scope.

## Logistics and supply chains

Shipment, inventory, and order status change across systems. The solution can assemble context, analyze an exception, and prepare follow-up with the time of the last update and any prediction limits.

Changes to a date, order, or route require defined execution policy, approval, and effect verification. Expected outputs identify status, source, exception, and next step.

## Choose an appropriate start

Look for recurring work, usable data, and an owner who can evaluate the result. Two sectors may start with the same functional solution while their rules, sources, and decision boundaries differ.

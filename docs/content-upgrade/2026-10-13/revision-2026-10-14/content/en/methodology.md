# Prove each step before expanding the scope

Agent engineering begins with the process and the result the customer needs. We define success and unacceptable outcomes, then build the system and its tests around that definition.

## Define the work

We review start and end points, inputs, source of truth, and decision responsibility. Recurring cases and consequential exceptions are recorded, along with what the system can propose or perform. The output is a task definition business and technical teams can discuss together.

## Assess data and systems

We review sources, versions, rights to use them, identity, integration interfaces, and processing location. This establishes what needs preparation, what can be tested with a bounded sample, and what stays outside scope.

## Build a bounded pilot

The first path covers the core task and selected incomplete-input and failure cases. Outputs are compared with reference cases. A plausible answer alone does not authorize external execution.

## Evaluate with process owners

We review source correctness, calculations, decisions, and output completeness. Testing includes ambiguous requests, changed information, tool failure, and unauthorized access. Results determine what needs repair and what can proceed to limited operation.

## Introduce operation gradually

A system may start in reading or drafting mode, then perform approved actions, then bounded autonomous actions where evaluation and policy support them. Conditions for suspension or returning to human review and exception ownership are defined.

## Deliver for use and further development

Agreed scope includes workflow definitions, integrations, tests, operating guidance, and maintenance responsibilities. Changes to knowledge, models, and tools follow versioned evaluation so an improvement in one area does not regress another task.

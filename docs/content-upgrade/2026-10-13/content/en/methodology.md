# How a task description becomes a testable system

The delivery process connects business requirements with implementation decisions. Each stage produces work your team can review before broader access or higher-impact actions.

## Define the task and scope

We examine requests as they arrive today, the information or approvals they wait for, and who owns errors. One starting point is selected, with explicit inclusions and exclusions.

Output: a workflow map, responsibilities, fictional or de-identified cases, and a baseline for the current process. Discovery does not require copying every company document.

[First workflow guide](/en/resources/first-workflow)

## Design knowledge, permissions and outputs

We identify the authoritative source, version and update rule for each input. Reading, proposing, approving and executing receive separate permissions. The output specification lets the reviewer determine whether the task is complete.

## Build a bounded pilot

The pilot uses only the data and tools required for the chosen task. Outputs are tested in an appropriate environment, and external actions receive approval gates where needed. Access or business-rule changes return to design review.

## Test acceptance and exceptions

Cases include a complete request, missing information, conflicting sources, unauthorized access and a connection failure. Results are compared with recorded expectations, with failures and remedies documented. Expected results are not changed to fit an answer.

[Acceptance testing guide](/en/resources/acceptance-testing)

## Controlled release and operating ownership

We specify who monitors records, who can stop operation, how changes are reviewed and what support covers. Expansion follows usage and test results rather than the success of an initial demo.

Duration, cost and deliverables follow an assessment of systems and data. There is no universal delivery date or promise to connect an untested system.

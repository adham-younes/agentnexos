# Logistics: handle exceptions from the latest reliable status

Missing updates and conflicting status consume operations and service time. Focus on assembling shipment context, verification needs and escalation while operational decisions remain with the owner.

## Status needs a source and timestamp

An internal record may show an old date after the carrier updates it. Define authoritative status, timing and what may be communicated before confirmation. An estimate does not become a delivery promise.

## An exception file the team can act on

The proposed system collects shipment ID, recent events, contact attempts and the issue, then routes the next step. Conflicting sources are presented for verification rather than selecting the most convenient account.

Illustrative example: no update arrives after dispatch. The system prepares a verification request with available details and a response that distinguishes confirmed status from pending information.

## Coordination does not authorize rescheduling

Route, date or address changes and claims settlements require their own permissions and policies. Scope defines access to contact and address data. Tracking hardware or physical-system integration is not assumed.

## Test conflict, outages and escalation

Choose a reviewable exception type and test delayed sources, outages and unowned cases. An acceptable output includes dated references and an owner, or an explained stop when evidence is insufficient.
